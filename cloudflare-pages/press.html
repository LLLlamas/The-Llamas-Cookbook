Description · 580 chars
Llamas Cookbook is an iPhone recipe app built around two ideas: your kitchen is shared, and recipes come from anywhere. Cook up to four recipes simultaneously, each with its own active state in the Dynamic Island. Save recipes from cookbook photos, links, iMessage forwards, and Notes-app jots — parsed on your device. Add friends, each with their own color, and see who's cooking right now. Browse a friend's library and import recipes you'd actually cook, with attribution that travels through the chain. Share any recipe via Messages and the recipient opens it in Safari — no app required. Free to download. Optional Llama Pro for expanded photo imports. iPhone only.

Long-form summary

Most recipe apps were designed for one job: bookmarking recipes from food blogs. Llamas Cookbook was designed for the moment you're actually cooking — and for how recipes move between people who know each other.

The headline feature is a Cook Mode that handles up to four recipes simultaneously. Each cook has its own steps, ingredients, and timer, and each active timer shows up in the Dynamic Island. Most recipe apps let you cook one thing; this one assumes you're juggling.

Recipes can be saved from almost anywhere: a cookbook photo (scan the page with the camera), a TikTok or Instagram caption, a recipe blog URL, an iMessage someone forwarded you, or a jot from the Notes app. The parsing happens on your device — no recipe data leaves the phone for text-based imports.

Friends in Llamas Cookbook aren't a social feed. Each friend has their own color in your library. A small pulse tells you who's cooking right now, and a quick glance shows what they last made. You can open any friend's cookbook, browse it, and import the recipes you'd actually cook. Imports carry an attribution chain — your grandma's lasagna keeps her name on it, even three friends down. When a friend imports your recipe, you get a silent ping.

Sharing works in both directions. Send any recipe via Messages and the recipient opens a clean URL in Safari — photos, ingredients, steps, no app required.

The app also ships a serving scaler, conversions between cups, grams, and ounces, a sourdough hydration calculator, and starter estimates. Recipes support a hero photo plus a photo at every step, so the loaf rise, the crumb, the plating — all of it stays attached to the recipe you cooked.

Llamas Cookbook is free to download with no advertising and no tracking. Llama Pro, an optional subscription, expands the monthly photo-import quota and unlocks additional power-user features, with more capabilities in development.

What the app does

Six things worth showing in a review. None of them require an explainer paragraph — they each do what they say.

🍲🍞

Cook four at once.

Up to four recipes can be active in Cook Mode simultaneously, each with its own steps, ingredients, and timer. Every active cook shows up in the Dynamic Island so you can put your phone on the counter and stop tapping.

👋

Friends, present.

Each friend has their own color in your library — a personal hex value that follows them through every recipe attribution and shared moment. A small pulse on their avatar tells you they're cooking right now. A line below shows what they last made.

📖

Borrow from a friend.

Open any friend's cookbook, scroll through what they cook, and import the recipes you'd actually make. Each import is a deep copy — yours to edit — but the attribution chain stays attached. Three friends down the line, grandma's name is still on the lasagna.

🪄

Save from anywhere.

A photo of a cookbook page. A TikTok or Instagram caption. A recipe blog URL. An iMessage someone forwarded you. A jot from the Notes app. All five paths import into a clean structured recipe — and most of the parsing happens on the device.

📸

Make it yours.

Add a hero photo. Add a photo to every step. Edit anything that came in from an import. Show the bulk-ferment rise, the crumb shot, the plating — the recipe becomes a record of the last time you cooked it.

⚖️

Built for craft.

Scale any recipe to a different serving size. Convert cups, grams, and ounces inline. A sourdough hydration calculator and starter-weight estimates for the bakers. Per-recipe notes and timers that ring through Silent mode.

How it's different

Most recipe apps in 2026 do a lot of the same things — save recipes from social media, organize a library, run a single cook timer. The category is well-established and well-served.

Llamas Cookbook is built around two ideas the rest of the field mostly misses. The first: when you actually cook, you cook more than one thing at once. Most apps treat cooking as a single-track activity. This one assumes you're managing four pots and an oven, and the Cook Mode + Dynamic Island make that feel orderly instead of frantic. The second: recipes are inherently social. They come from people, they go to people, and the trail of where they've been is part of what makes them yours. Pestle's "households" and Mela's "shared library" both treat sharing as one pooled cookbook. Llamas Cookbook treats it like a real friend graph — each friend in their own color, each recipe carrying its history of who passed it along.

Everything else — the photo and link imports, the share-to-anyone-without-the-app flow, the sourdough math, the per-step photos — is the supporting cast. Free to download, with an optional Llama Pro subscription for power users who want expanded photo-import volume and more features as they ship.

About the developer

Lorenzo is a solo developer based in the US. He built Llamas Cookbook over two years out of frustration with recipes scattered across screenshots, browser tabs, and the iOS Notes app. The app ships small improvements every week or two — what's in users' hands today won't be the same app a month from now.

The llama mascot is built into the brand: every other recipe app is faceless, and Llamas Cookbook isn't. The character appears throughout the UI as a progress indicator, share-preview watermark, and Cook Mode companion.
